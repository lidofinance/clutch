Clutch: how the DAO's active treasury vault works

An onboarding guide for signers, engineers, reviewers and everyone who needs the picture.

Hypothetical draft. This page shows a design under discussion for an active treasury vault of the Lido DAO. Nothing is deployed, and nothing is ratified: no Lido body has approved it, and it authorises no action. Every number, address and parameter is a draft that can change. Scene 1 of 10

The vault, and who owns it

Clutch puts a bounded part of the DAO treasury to work in approved protocols. The assets sit in one new Safe, the Asset Safe.

The Asset Safe has exactly one owner: the Aragon Agent, the DAO's treasury. The DAO keeps custody. In an emergency, assets go back to the Agent, a fixed address.

The Agent also owns two smaller Safes: the orders account, which places swap orders, and the first-loss Safe, which holds the DAO's first-loss shares apart.

The Asset Safe is a Safe v1.5.0 proxy. The Aragon Agent is its only owner, at a threshold of one, and it has no guard and no fallback handler. The Agent approves each transaction by approveHash, or sends it itself (INV-001).

Scene 2 of 10

Nobody touches the vault directly

The Asset Safe switches on two Roles modifiers from Zodiac. Each holder of a role calls a modifier. The modifier checks the call against the policy and, if it is allowed, runs it as the Asset Safe.

The policy is default-deny: a call that the policy does not list is refused.

The modifiers are EIP-1167 proxies of the deployed, locked Roles mastercopy. The Asset Safe is their owner, avatar and target.

Scene 3 of 10

The operator: the committee at work

The Treasury Management Committee runs the vault through a new operator Safe, at 4 of 7. The operator can enter and leave approved protocols, within budgets.

It cannot move assets out: every receiver, owner and beneficiary in its permissions is the Asset Safe.

The operator Safe calls execTransactionWithRole on the operator modifier with the role key bytes32("operator"); a batch goes through MultiSendCallOnly v1.5.0. Every receiver, owner and beneficiary is pinned with EqualToAvatar (INV-003).

Scene 4 of 10

Every operator transaction is screened

[the screening vendor]'s guard sits on the operator Safe. It refuses any transaction that the vendor has not approved, once and exactly.

If the vendor stops, the operator stops too: the guard fails closed. Recovery does not change, because no guard sits on the emergency path.

At launch the guard is not set yet. The vault starts under a launch cap of one million dollars, and the cap lifts once the guard is live (OD-54).

A transaction guard on the operator Safe. The vendor's key approves each exact transaction on chain, once, before it runs; a transaction without an approval reverts. The owners can remove the guard only through a 10-day timelock. Its bypass mode stays off: turning it on needs the vendor's approval and a 10-day timelock (INV-010).

Scene 5 of 10

Changes by motion, inside templates

The committee can widen its own permissions only through Easy Track motions, each built by a fixed template. LDO holders have 72 hours to object, and 0.5% of LDO stops a motion. This window is the timelock for every structural increase of exposure, such as a new target or a higher budget.

At enactment, the governance role writes the change. The modifier checks that write again: no role change, no delegatecall, and no administrative target. The Asset Safe and its modifiers are refused as targets. The same motion gives the emergency role an exit from what it onboards.

The governance role may call six functions on the operator modifier: scopeTarget, revokeTarget, allowFunction, revokeFunction, scopeFunction and setAllowance. The role key is pinned to operator, and a Nor condition refuses the Asset Safe and its modules as targets. The execution option must be below 2, so no delegatecall. setAllowance takes only the operator's budget keys, with a refill period of at least 30 days (INV-004, INV-012).

Scene 6 of 10

Swaps are CoW orders

Every swap is a CoW order placed from the orders account. The operator sends a token there under a budget, then places a market, limit, TWAP or stop-loss order that pays the Asset Safe. A recovery order buys USDC or USDT and pays the Aragon Agent.

Nothing on chain bounds an order's price. CoW's competition rules, screening, the budgets and monitoring of each fill take its place.

Market and limit orders: a delegatecall from the orders account to CoW's order signer, signOrder, with tokens from the lists, the Asset Safe as receiver and a validDuration of at most 30 days. TWAP and stop-loss orders go through ComposableCoW, with only those two handlers; a TWAP order is created with createWithContext, so it starts at its creation. These permissions are ADR 007's proposed direction (INV-022, INV-024).

Scene 7 of 10

Money in: top-ups

Funding comes from the DAO treasury through the existing payment path. Once a month, the operator Safe creates a top-up motion. LDO holders can object for 72 hours.

Two funding registries pay only the Asset Safe, each within a monthly limit. The objection is the control; the limit is the backstop.

Two allowed-recipients registries from Easy Track's standard builder, each with the Asset Safe as its only recipient and a one-month period. Two top-up factories create the motions, with the operator Safe as their trusted caller (INV-017).

Scene 8 of 10

Emergencies: two fast stops, one slow exit

The emergency Safe has the committee's signers at 2 of 7. Through the safety modifier it revokes the operator, exits positions and sends assets to the Aragon Agent. It is fast, but it is not independent of the committee.

The Emergency Brakes multisig, outside the committee, can switch the operator modifier off and pause Easy Track. Only the DAO is fully independent, and a DAO vote takes days.

Target: initiate any emergency action within six hours. Drills on a fork must prove it before launch.

Scene 9 of 10

Caps are watched, not enforced

No contract reverts on a cap or on the liquidity buffer. Monitoring watches the buffer all the time and publishes the cap reading to IPFS every two weeks. Each month, the committee publishes a report that anyone can reproduce.

If a breach is not fixed within two working days and is larger at the next reading, monitoring pages the Emergency Brakes multisig, which switches the operator off. The control matrix lists each rule's controls on every layer.

The cap reading is the report generator's output at the fortnightly snapshot's pinned block, published to IPFS. The alert that pages the Emergency Brakes multisig links it (OD-32).

Scene 10 of 10

Little new code, and where it stands

Clutch reuses deployed code that already runs: Safe, Zodiac Roles, Easy Track, CoW's order contracts and Lido's monitoring. It adds Easy Track factories, the permission policy with its compiler, the vault's detectors and a report generator.

Today the design is in phase 0. Nothing is deployed, no audit is done and no runbook is written. The Built or reused and Status tabs show each part.

New code: the Easy Track factories, Clutch's TypeScript policy compiler, the detectors and the report generator. Reused as deployed: Safe v1.5.0, the locked Roles mastercopy, Easy Track and CoW's contracts.

What the committee does on a normal day. The policy checks every operator transaction before it runs, and so does the screening guard once the launch cap lifts (OD-54).

An operator transaction

✓ Built and tested on a fork

How the committee changes a position, for example a USDS deposit into sUSDS. The screening guard and the policy both check the transaction before it runs.

Trigger The committee decides to change a position within the mandate. Time Minutes, after [the screening vendor] approves.

  1. Operator Safe signers, 4 of 7

    The signers build the transaction in the Zodiac UI or with local tooling. It calls the operator modifier with the operator role.

    The call is execTransactionWithRole on the operator modifier, with the role key bytes32("operator"). A batch goes through MultiSendCallOnly v1.5.0.

  2. [the screening vendor]

    The vendor reviews the exact transaction and approves it on chain, once. Without the approval, the guard makes the operator Safe refuse it.

    Check The guard fails closed. Until the launch cap lifts, no guard is set and this step does not happen (OD-54). INV-010 is pending: the harness has no guard yet.

  3. Operator modifier

    The modifier checks the call against the operator's permissions and refuses anything that the policy does not list. An approval spends its spender's budget key.

  4. Asset Safe

    The modifier runs the call as the Asset Safe. The protocol pays any proceeds or claims to the Asset Safe, because every receiver is pinned to it.

    Check INV-003: every receiver, owner and beneficiary is the Asset Safe.

  5. Monitoring

    Monitoring records the change: approvals, budget burn and positions.

    Check Specified: the vault's detectors are not built.

If this goes wrong
  • The vendor refuses: nothing runs. The committee fixes the transaction or asks again.
  • The policy refuses: the call reverts and nothing changes.
  • A problem shows after the call: the emergency Safe revokes the operator at once.

A market or limit order

◐ Specified, not built

How the vault swaps one token for another. The operator sends the token to the orders account and places a CoW order there. The order pays the Asset Safe, because the policy pins the receiver.

Trigger The committee wants a different mix of assets, for example less stETH and more USDC, now or at a set price. Time A market order fills in minutes. A limit order can stand for up to 30 days (OD-47).

  1. Operator

    The operator transfers the token from the Asset Safe to the orders account. The transfer spends that token's orders budget, and the policy pins the recipient.

    Check Specified: the orders account and its permissions are not in the policy yet.

  2. Operator Safe

    On the orders account, the operator Safe signs a market or limit order through CoW's order signer. The screening guard checks this transaction too.

    Check INV-022: the order's receiver is the Asset Safe.

A TWAP or stop-loss order

◐ Specified, not built

How the vault buys or sells over time, or sells when a price falls. ComposableCoW holds the order, and CoW's watch-tower posts it to the order book.

Trigger The committee wants to average into a position over weeks, or to sell stETH if its price falls to a set level. Time Until the last part fills or the order expires.

  1. Operator

    The operator funds the orders account from the Asset Safe, under the orders budget.

  2. Operator Safe

    The operator Safe creates the order on ComposableCoW: a TWAP order in equal parts, or a stop-loss order with a strike. A TWAP order starts at once and ends within 30 days. The screening guard checks it, and is asked to refuse a stop-loss order that expires more than 30 days ahead.

    Check INV-022: the receiver is the Asset Safe, and a stop-loss order's oracles are Chainlink feeds that the policy pins.

    ComposableCoW.createWithContext with a start of zero and CoW's current-timestamp value factory, so the order starts at its creation, and the handler pinned to the deployed TWAP or StopLoss handler. A TWAP order has at most 30 parts at most a day apart, or 4 parts at most 7 days apart (INV-024). The orders account's fallback handler is CoW's ExtensibleFallbackHandler, and ComposableCoW verifies CoW's settlement domain for it (INV-023).

  3. CoW's watch-tower

    The watch-tower posts each part of a TWAP order when it is due, or a stop-loss order once its price is met.

  4. CoW Protocol

    Each part fills at its minimum or better, and pays the Asset Safe directly.

  5. Monitoring

    Monitoring checks each fill, and alerts when a part is not posted on time.

If this goes wrong
  • The watch-tower stops: the orders wait and no funds move. The committee can remove them with remove and place ordinary orders.
  • The emergency Safe can cancel every open order and sweep the orders account.

Onboarding a new protocol by motion

◐ Specified, not built

How the committee adds a protocol without a full DAO vote. It creates an Easy Track motion through a fixed template, and LDO holders have 72 hours to object. The policy's limits on the governance role are built and tested on a fork; the template factories are not built.

Trigger The committee wants a new target, for example Lido Lend after its launch. Time At least 72 hours, then enactment.

  1. Committee

    The committee posts a disclosure on the forum. It sets up monitoring for the new target and updates the runbooks.

  2. Operator Safe

    The operator Safe creates the motion on the template factory, with typed values only: a target, an asset and a budget key. The screening guard checks this transaction.

  3. LDO holders

    The motion waits 72 hours. If holders of 0.5% of LDO object, it fails. This window is the timelock for every structural increase of exposure. The Emergency Brakes multisig can also pause Easy Track.

  4. Easy Track

    At enactment, Easy Track rebuilds the script and requires the hash stored at creation. A changed script fails.

    Check INV-005, tested on a mock Easy Track.

  5. Governance role

    The script executor writes the new operator permission through the governance role. The modifier checks this write too: the operator key only, no role change, no delegatecall and no administrative target.

    Check INV-004: the Asset Safe and every module of it are refused as targets (OD-38).

  6. Exit-governance role

    The same script gives the emergency role the new target's exits on the safety modifier: redeem, withdraw, claim and cancel pinned to the Asset Safe, a transfer pinned to the Agent, and an approval set to zero.

    Check INV-025: this role writes only under the emergency key, with no delegatecall, allowance or administrative target. Specified, not built (OD-49).

  7. Operator, and the emergency Safe if needed

    The operator can use the new permission. The emergency Safe can revoke it at once.

If this goes wrong
  • An objection of 0.5% of LDO stops the motion.
  • The Emergency Brakes multisig pauses Easy Track. Only the DAO can unpause it.
  • The operator Safe cancels its own motion.
  • After enactment, the emergency Safe revokes the new operator permission at once. A write to the safety modifier cannot be revoked that way: only an objection, the Easy Track pause or a DAO vote stops it (OD-49).

The four properties

DAO ownership

The Aragon Agent is the only owner of every account that holds assets. The Asset Safe owns both of its modifiers, which are proxies of a locked mastercopy.

Sources ADR 005 INV-001 INV-021 INV-023

Default-deny permissions

Each role reaches only named targets and functions, and every receiver is pinned to the vault. A motion adds a permission only through a template fixed at audit time, and never grants delegatecall or administration.

Sources ADR 005 ADR 006 INV-003 INV-004

Rapid risk reduction

The emergency Safe revokes, exits and returns assets at once, and no guard sits on its path. The Emergency Brakes multisig can switch the operator off. Fork drills must show each action started within six hours.

Sources ADR 005 INV-002 INV-009 INV-010 ROADMAP

Walk-away operation

Clutch compiles and checks the policy itself, from the reviewed commit, and the provider's hosted app is out of the path. Removal, recovery and DAO actions are plain on-chain calls with no vendor code on their path.

Sources ADR 004 OD-33 INV-010

Governance and Easy Track

  • ↻ Changed

    The RFP asked Easy Track manages the operator allowlist: it adds or removes an asset, a protocol target or a function selector, and changes a parameter constraint inside an allowed function.

    Clutch Motions only add. An onboarding motion fills one of five templates, fixed at audit time, with typed parameters such as a target, an asset and a budget key, and a budget motion sets budgets within their ceilings. A removal is the emergency Safe's immediate revoke, and a new token for the orders account needs a DAO vote.

    Why A motion that carried its own condition tree could widen authority past what the DAO approved. A removal by motion would wait at least 48 hours, because Easy Track cannot skip its objection window.

    Sources ADR 006 OD-09 OD-46 INV-004

  • ✓ As asked

    Clutch The Easy Track factories are the only new contracts. Lido builds them in phase 2, and an external audit covers them in phase 3. The governance role calls six administration functions of the Roles modifier, each one guarded.

    Sources ADR 006 INV-004 ROADMAP

    Clutch Easy Track never receives authority over the Agent. Its executor holds only the governance and exit-governance roles, and the operator Safe, as the trusted caller of every factory, creates every motion.

1 No role can reach an owner-only function of the Asset Safe, the orders account or a modifier.

Design

The Asset Safe owns both modifiers, and every role call runs as the Asset Safe. The emergency revoke and the governance writes work only because a narrow role may administer a modifier. The LIP calls this composition the primary risk.

If it is false

An operator, a motion or a factory bug gets administration of the vault. Fork probes found two such paths, and both are closed: a motion could grant the operator delegatecall (OD-36), or give it the safety modifier as a target (OD-38).

A numbered decision record in docs/adr/. An agent drafts it. It stays proposed until EM, and later the committee, accepts it. An acceptance authorises nothing on chain.

The Zodiac name for a budget key.

Permission for a named contract, the spender, to pull tokens from the Safe. In Clutch, an approval to a protocol spends that spender's budget key or stays under a fixed ceiling. An approval of zero is always allowed.

Aragon Agent

The DAO's treasury contract and its final authority. It executes what DAO votes decide, and it is the only owner of the Asset Safe, the orders account and the first-loss Safe.

The committed JSON file that reviewers approve. It holds the modifier calls in order, the expected condition tree for every role, target and function, and the budget keys.

Attested computation

A calculation that actually ran on pinned inputs, checked by an independent attester. It is the only allowed source of a financial figure.

Avatar

The account that a modifier acts for: here, the Asset Safe. "Pinned to the avatar" means that a receiver, owner or beneficiary must be the Asset Safe.

Budget key

A refillable spending quota on the operator modifier, counted in token units. A permitted call draws it down, and it refills each period up to a maximum.

A Zodiac allowance: balance, maxRefill, refill, period and timestamp under a 32-byte key. The key is the label in bytes, such as bytes32("earn_usd_deposit").

Cap

A mandate limit on exposure, written as a share of a defined base. Measurement, publication and remediation keep it. No contract reverts on a cap.

Compiler

Clutch's own TypeScript program on the Zodiac roles SDK. It turns the constellation into the artifact offline, and it refuses entries that only the hosted app can compile.

A CoW order that ComposableCoW holds and checks, such as a TWAP or a stop-loss order. CoW's watch-tower posts it to the order book when it is due.

The Zodiac description of the Safes, the modifiers, the roles, the permissions and the budget keys, written in TypeScript under policy/constellation/. It is the source of Clutch's permission policy.
